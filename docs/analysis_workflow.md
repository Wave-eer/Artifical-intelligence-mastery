# Brent Crude Oil Price Analysis Workflow & Methodological Framework

## 1. Analysis Workflow Overview

The objective of this pipeline is to analyze historical Brent Crude spot price data (May 1987 to September 2022), detect structural break/change points using Bayesian MCMC modeling (PyMC), quantify regime parameter shifts (mean price, volatility, log return distributions), and link detected change points to key geopolitical, OPEC, and macroeconomic events.

### Phase 1: Data Preparation & Preprocessing
- **Source**: Daily Brent crude oil spot prices from FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis).
- **Date Formatting & Normalization**: Parsing custom date formats (`DD-MMM-YY` to `YYYY-MM-DD` standard datetimes).
- **Log Return Calculation**: Computing daily percentage price changes and logarithmic returns $r_t = \ln(P_t / P_{t-1})$ to satisfy stationarity requirements.
- **Event Alignment**: Compiling a structured event dataset (`data/brent_events.csv`) covering 14 major historical shocks (Gulf War, Lehman Crisis, 2014 OPEC Price War, COVID-19 pandemic, Russia-Ukraine war).

### Phase 2: Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
- **Trend & Cycle Decomposition**: Plotting 35-year raw price series and log returns.
- **Stationarity Testing**: Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) test on raw prices vs. log returns.
- **Volatility Dynamics**: Rolling 30-day and 90-day standard deviations to identify high-volatility regimes.

### Phase 3: Bayesian Change Point Modeling (PyMC)
- **Model Specification**:
  - Discrete switch point $\tau \sim \text{DiscreteUniform}(0, N-1)$ representing the index in time where a regime change occurs.
  - Early and late mean parameters $\mu_1, \mu_2 \sim \text{Normal}(\mu_P, \sigma_P)$.
  - Early and late standard deviations $\sigma_1, \sigma_2 \sim \text{HalfNormal}(1.0)$ or $\text{Exponential}(1.0)$.
  - Deterministic step function via `pm.math.switch(tau >= idx, mu1, mu2)` and `pm.math.switch(tau >= idx, sigma1, sigma2)`.
  - Normal likelihood $P_t \sim \text{Normal}(\mu_t, \sigma_t)$.
- **MCMC Sampling & Diagnostics**:
  - Sampling via PyMC (`pm.sample(draws=1000, tune=1000, chains=2)`).
  - Convergence diagnostics: $R_{\hat{}}$ (Gelman-Rubin statistic, requiring $R_{\hat{}} < 1.05$), trace plots, effective sample size ($ESS$).
- **Posterior HDI Extraction**:
  - 95% Highest Density Interval (HDI) for $\tau$ mapped to exact calendar date ranges.

### Phase 4: Event Impact & Interpretation
- Mapping detected $\tau$ posterior distribution to historical events.
- Quantifying percentage shifts in mean price ($\Delta \mu = \mu_2 - \mu_1$) and volatility changes across regimes.

## 2. Assumptions & Methodological Limitations

> [!IMPORTANT]  
> **Correlation vs. Causation Constraint**: Bayesian change point detection identifies statistical regime shifts in time-series data ($\tau$), but **does not prove direct causal mechanics**. While a detected change point may closely coincide with a major geopolitical event (e.g., the November 2014 OPEC decision or March 2020 COVID-19 lockdown), market prices are driven by complex, multi-factor macroeconomic dynamics, supply chain expectations, and financial speculation.

### Core Assumptions:
1. **Single or Discrete Switch Points**: The primary model assumes a step change at index $\tau$, partitioning the time series into distinct homogeneous statistical regimes.
2. **Stationarity of Sub-regimes**: Sub-regimes before and after $\tau$ are assumed to be generated by consistent underlying stochastic processes.
3. **Data Completeness**: Price data missing non-trading days (weekends/holidays) are treated sequentially by observation index.

### Limitations:
- **Exogenous Variables**: The baseline change point model operates strictly on univariate price series without explicit exogenous covariates.
- **Overlapping Events**: In periods of rapid economic shifts (e.g., Spring 2020 COVID-19 pandemic + OPEC+ price war), change point models isolate the net statistical shift, requiring domain knowledge to disambiguate concurrent catalysts.
